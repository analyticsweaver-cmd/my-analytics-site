What The Weaver Line Is Actually Doing

July 24, 2026

Every week, The Weaver Line spits out a power rating for 136 college football teams and a predicted margin for every matchup on the board. Here's what's actually happening under the hood, and why it sometimes disagrees hard with Vegas.

Start with the power rating. It's built from a handful of per-play numbers: success rate (how often a play keeps a drive on schedule, roughly half the needed yards on first down, all of it by third), explosiveness (how many yards you gain on the plays that do work), and havoc rate (how often the defense blows up a play with a tackle for loss, a forced fumble, or a pick). Offense and defense each get scored on all three, adjusted for who the team actually played. Ohio State sits at the top right now, with Indiana close behind, and both show up near the top of SP+ too, an outside power rating the model checks itself against rather than builds from.

Early in the season, there aren't enough real games to lean on, so the rating leans on a prior instead. That prior blends returning production, transfer portal activity, and coaching continuity. A first-time head coach costs more than a coordinator handoff. As real games pile up, that prior fades and the on-field numbers take over.

The part that actually matters for picking games is Model Edge: the gap between what the model predicts and what the market spread implies. In week 10, the model had Oklahoma State beating Kansas by a lot more than the market did, an 18-point edge. That's the model flagging a mispriced game, not proof it's correct. Home Field Edge gets folded in too, and it isn't one flat number for every stadium. It's specific to how that team has actually performed at home.

None of this is a betting service. It's a personal model, built and re-tuned by hand, and the honest weak spot is right there in the preseason prior: those are judgment calls about coaching and roster turnover, not something computed from stats. The model is most useful exactly where it disagrees with the crowd and turns out to be right, and least useful in the first couple weeks of a season when the prior is doing most of the talking.
