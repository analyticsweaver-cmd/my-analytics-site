How to read this page

Every prediction here comes from one number per team, the Power Score — built from three pieces: Baseline (last year's record), Trajectory (a hand-scored read on roster needs and off-field stability), and Regression (a check on whether a team's record matched how good their point differential said they actually were). Coaching continuity is scored for every team too, but a July audit found it wasn't adding real predictive signal, so it's tracked and shown, not counted toward the number — see Methodology.

Every team also gets a Cone of Certainty — a projected win-total range, not just a single guess — and every division gets a Confidence Rating for how close the race at the top actually is.

Full formulas and sources are in the Methodology & Sources section at the bottom. The NFL dashboard also has a glossary covering every term used there in more depth.

Rookie Waiting

Spotlight: Cleveland's Four-Man Room

The most chaotic QB room in the league entering training camp — no presumed favorite among four real options.

Deshaun Watson — Veteran

Clemson. 72 regular-season starts, three-time Pro Bowler. Recovering from a ruptured Achilles — no 2025 regular-season snaps logged.

Superpower: playoff-tested arm talent and mobility when healthy.
Weakness: extensive injury history — availability is the real question, not talent.

Shedeur Sanders — 2025 Rookie

Colorado. 2025 draft: Round 5, Pick 144. 120/212, 1,400 yds, 7 TD, 10 INT, 3-4 as a starter, 18.8 QBR (ESPN's 0–100 quarterback rating — above 50 is roughly average).

Superpower: deep-ball accuracy and big-play ability — four 50+-yard completions in his first three starts.
Weakness: turnover-prone; holds the ball too long against NFL pass rush.

Dillon Gabriel — 2025 Rookie

Oregon. 2025 draft: Round 3, Pick 94; FBS all-time leader in total TDs (189). 110/185, 937 yds, 7 TD, 2 INT, 1-5 as a starter, 80.8 rating.

Superpower: ball security — lowest INT rate of the three; most career starts in FBS history.
Weakness: lacks downfield explosiveness — among the league's lowest EPA/dropback in 2025 (a per-play efficiency stat — higher is better).

Taylen Green — 2026 Rookie

Arkansas (transfer from Boise State). 2026 draft: Round 6, Pick 182. 2025 college: 2,714 yds, 19 TD, 11 INT — broke NFL Combine vertical & broad jump records at 6'6"/224.

Superpower: elite size, arm talent, and athleticism — rare physical upside for the position.
Weakness: raw decision-making and operational polish — graded a Day 3 developmental project.

The Injury Picture Entering Camp

Five situations carrying real, sourced medical status into training camp — not soft narrative, actual reported updates already baked into the model.

Chiefs Major

Mahomes tore his ACL + LCL in Week 15. Week 1 2026 availability is genuinely uncertain despite ahead-of-schedule rehab reports — KC added Kenneth Walker III to reduce pressure on his return.

Packers Major

Parsons' ACL + meniscus recovery is now reported to likely cost him much of the regular season — not cleared for practice until September, targeting a postseason return.

Lions Major

S Kerby Joseph's status is "team does not know when or if he will return" per HC Dan Campbell — worse than a recovery-timeline question.

Seahawks Moderate

RB Zach Charbonnet's ACL recovery could cost him the start of the season — newly reported, not previously priced in.

Colts Moderate

WR Alec Pierce's ankle surgery carries a 4-6 month recovery that could cost him the preseason and part of the regular season.

The Model vs. the Market

Sportsbooks price all 32 teams too. Lining our order up against theirs shows where the model is making a claim the market has not, and where it is simply agreeing with the consensus.

Ranks compared, not raw units — a Power Score and a win total are different scales. Positive gap = the model is higher on a team than the market.

Biggest disagreement

Chiefs — market T3 of 32 (10.5), model 26th

The market still has Kansas City in the top handful of win totals in football. The model has them 26th, on an aging core around a 36-year-old Kelce, Rice's suspension risk, and — the acute piece — Mahomes' own ACL+LCL recovery with Week 1 availability genuinely uncertain. One of these reads is badly wrong.

Where the model is early

Lions 10.5 · Ravens 11.5

Both sit near the top of the market and well outside the model's top half — Lions 23rd, Ravens 20th. Detroit's gap is specific: Kerby Joseph's status is "team does not know when or if he will return," on top of an Achilles tear and an arrest already in the secondary. Baltimore's is roster-driven, not injury-driven — losing All-Pro center Linderbaum to career backups, under a full staff overhaul. Neither line has moved to reflect it.

Where the model is long

Jaguars 8.5 · Saints 7.5 · Broncos 9.5

Three teams the model ranks in its top nine (Jaguars tied for 4th, Saints 8th, Broncos 1st) while the market prices them well down the board. All three carry zero or near-zero coaching turnover, which the Trajectory score rewards more heavily than the board does.

Methodology & Sources

Methodology at a Glance

Trajectory Score

Need-Fill + Stability

Baseline Score

(2025 Wins − 8.5) × 2

Regression Score

−(Actual Wins − Pythagorean Expected Wins) × 3

Power Score

(0.45 × Baseline) + (0.35 × Trajectory) + (0.20 × Regression)

Cone of Certainty

Power Score ± (Upside/Downside, scored 0–10 each)

Confidence Rating

Gap(#1 vs #2) − Avg. Volatility(#1,#2) ÷ 6

Scheme Continuity (coaching-staff turnover) is still scored and shown in every team's rationale, but a July 24 audit found it added no real predictive signal once tested against three seasons of actual results — it's tracked, not counted toward Trajectory or Power Score. As of August 2026, every Power Score display on this page (Cone of Certainty, Division Confidence, Deep Dives, the Risk Map, and the Model vs. Market table) has been rescored to actually match this formula — some had drifted back to including Scheme Continuity after the July audit.
