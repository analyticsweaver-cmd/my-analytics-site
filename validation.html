BenchmarksWhat we're actually being measured against

A naive "always predict 50/50" baseline scores 0.25 on the Brier scale — both models clear that by a real margin, not a marginal one. The more honest comparison is against Vegas: closing lines, checked against the NFL model on 816 real games, are noticeably more accurate. Expected — the market prices in everything up to kickoff, including injury news, and this model only updates when the pipeline is re-run by hand. Neither model ingests betting lines, so there's no line-shopping trick making either one look artificially sharp.

Biggest misses, and what we did about themCaught, not hidden

NFL — found and fixed

Tail overconfidence. The model's win probabilities were checked against 816 real games and came back overconfident at the extremes: games called at 76.5% actually got won 68.1% of the time. Fix: a 20,000-run Monte Carlo simulation marginalizes over strength uncertainty instead of evaluating a single point estimate — this is the same fix behind the Cone of Certainty. Now calibrated (Brier 0.2375, down from 0.2497 naive).

NFL — found and fixed

Coaching continuity was quietly hurting accuracy. A 2026-07-24 audit found that "Scheme Continuity" — whether a team kept its coordinators — was actually making Power Score worse, not better. Three independent checks agreed. Removing it from the formula improved the season-level win correlation from r = 0.352 to r = 0.382. Scheme is still scored and shown on team pages; it just no longer moves the number.

CFB — found and fixed

A coaching-change signal was silently doing nothing. Pooling three seasons together (instead of validating one at a time) surfaced that the coaching-change feature had never actually been wired into the multi-season backtest — every run scored it as if no team ever changed coaches. Separately, pooling exposed real multicollinearity in the matchup model (three coefficients flipped sign between single-season and pooled fits); fixed by dropping the redundant term.

CFB — checked, honest negative result

The run/pass "fragility" flag doesn't predict prediction error. We checked whether teams flagged as unbalanced (too rush-reliant or pass-reliant) actually get missed more often by the model. They don't — the error difference is 0.244 points, against a standard error of 0.422, well within noise. The flag stays on team pages as a qualitative watchlist item; it's not wired into the rating, because it hasn't earned a place in it.
