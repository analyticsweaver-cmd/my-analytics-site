Methodology & Validation — Updated 2026-08-06

How the model works, and how it's been checked

Power Score (College Football) is a younger model than its NFL sibling, and this page is honest about that gap while it closes. It's now backtested across three full seasons, has a calibrated win-probability layer, and one qualitative flag was checked and found not to be backed by the data — reported as a real negative result, not hidden.

How it worksPower Score, in plain terms

Every team gets a single Power Score, an opponent-adjusted efficiency rating built from offensive and defensive success rate and explosiveness (rush and pass split out separately), shrunk early in the season toward a personnel and coaching prior that fades out as real games accumulate. A team-specific home-field edge (team_home_edge) is added for the home team — unlike the NFL model, which currently uses a flat league-wide number for every team (see its Methodology page).

Two matchup layers sit on top of the season-long rating: an aggregate model (Power Score difference, run through a fitted margin/win-probability curve) and a matchup-decomposed model that regresses the actual point margin on each side's specific rush/pass/havoc/coaching edges. The matchup model beats the aggregate one on pooled accuracy (MAE 13.65 vs. 13.71) — it earns its keep across seasons, not just the one it happened to be tuned on.

ValidationWhat's actually been tested

Three full seasons (2023–2025, 1,979 games) were backtested via walk-forward validation, pooled for real statistical power rather than trusted one season at a time. Two of the model's tuning constants were checked against all three seasons rather than assumed: RECENCY_DECAY turned out to be a placebo dial — MAE barely moves across its entire tested range, so it's now labeled a style choice, not a tuned parameter. SHRINKAGE_K is a real, reproduced finding — lower K wins on raw accuracy in every season tested, though the current default trades roughly 0.3 MAE points for better face validity (avoiding rankings like a Group-of-5 team above Georgia), a deliberate tradeoff, not an oversight.

Per-season stability was checked before this recalibration (at the previous 0.80/0.20 prior-weight default): 2023 13.45 MAE / 16.83 RMSE, 2024 13.91 / 17.89, 2025 14.06 / 17.72 — stable season to season, no wild swings. The numbers above reflect the current, recalibrated weights.

Win probability is calibrated directly against the pooled backtest: Brier score 0.208 against a naive always-50% baseline of 0.250 — a real, substantial improvement. Unlike the NFL model, this came back well-calibrated out of the box; the tail-overconfidence problem that required a Monte Carlo-averaging fix on the NFL side doesn't show up here, so no equivalent fix was needed.

Bugs found and fixedWhat changed on 2026-08-06, and why

Pooling three seasons together, rather than validating one at a time, surfaced two real problems that a single-season check had missed.

Fixed

Coaching-continuity signal was silently zero in every backtest run to date. The coaching-change signal (coaching_diff) was never actually threaded into the multi-season backtest — every run scored it as if no team ever changed coaches. Fixed and re-verified as part of the Phase 2 prior-weight recalibration below.

Fixed

A real multicollinearity problem in the matchup-decomposed model, caught by pooling. Three coefficients (success_diff, rush_diff, pass_diff) flipped sign between the single-season fit and a naive multi-season pooled fit — a risk the code's own docstring had already flagged as hypothetical but never checked. Fix: success_diff dropped entirely, letting rush_diff/pass_diff carry that signal — sign-stable across all three seasons pooled, with no accuracy cost.

Recalibrated

Personnel/coaching prior weight moved from 0.80/0.20 to 0.75/0.25 after a proper leave-one-season-out cross-validation (same method the NFL model uses for its own weight checks) — CV consistently favored less personnel weight than the old default, a small but real, consistent gain (pooled MAE 13.73 → 13.71, same direction every fold). Calibration was re-verified after the change, not assumed to still hold — it does, marginally tighter (Brier 0.2084 → 0.2080).

Checked, honest negative result

The run-reliant/pass-reliant fragility flag does not have a validated accuracy relationship. Fragile teams' backtest error is only 0.244 points higher than balanced teams' — against a standard error of 0.422, well within noise — and a continuous reliance-vs-error check found essentially zero correlation (r = -0.018). This is a legitimate "checked, and the answer is no" result, not a gap. The flag stays a qualitative watchlist tool on team pages; it was never wired into any model coefficient, so nothing about the live ratings changes because of this finding.

Model updateWhat changed on 2026-08-22, and why

Added

New coaching-quality signal added: recruiting-adjusted SP+ residual. The existing coaching-continuity signal only measured disruption (did the team change head coaches, and how severe a change). It said nothing about whether a coaching staff was actually good at outperforming its talent level. The new signal regresses each team's SP+ rating on its recruiting-talent composite across the whole FBS field per season, keeps the residual (over/under-performance relative to talent), z-scores it, and lags it one season so it can inform next season's rating without leaking that season's own results.

Validated with the same leave-one-season-out cross-validation discipline used elsewhere in this model: personnel/coaching/residual weights moved from 0.75/0.25/0.0 to 0.40/0.20/0.40, a real, consistent gain — every fold in the grid search converged on the same weights, not just a narrow margin in one season. Pooled MAE improved from 13.630 to 13.085 across all 1,979 games in the backtest. Re-verified independently after wiring into production (cfb_coaching_residual.py, plus the 3 backtest/validation scripts that call combine_priors()): full multiseason backtest on the live code path landed at pooled MAE 13.10, matching the validation number within ~0.1% — expected noise between two independently-computed code paths, not a discrepancy.

Known limitsWhat this model doesn't do yet

  • No Monte Carlo season simulation yet. The NFL model runs a 20,000-season simulation for win-total ranges and playoff odds; CFB doesn't have this infrastructure yet. The calibrated win-probability engine this would run on is done, so the remaining work is the simulation layer itself, not the underlying math.
  • No in-dashboard validation panel yet. The numbers on this page exist and are real; surfacing them inside the CFB dashboard itself (the way the NFL dashboard's "How well does this model actually work?" panel does) is in progress.
  • Need-Fill and Stability are hand-scored judgment, not formulas — same caveat as the NFL model's Trajectory inputs.
  • 2026 in-season accuracy work (opponent-adjusted stats, matchup win probability for played games) is blocked until real games exist — CFBD has no 2026 game data until kickoff, typically late August. The preseason baseline itself is current and real, refreshed 2026-08-06.
Every number on this page is backtested or measured against real historical data (2023–2025 seasons) — nothing here is an estimate standing in for something unmeasured. Full technical detail lives in CFB-Model-Validation-Roadmap.md in the project's internal notes.
