Methodology & Technical Audit — Updated 2026-07-24

How the model works, and how it's been checked

This page replaces the old inline validation blurb with the real thing: how Power Score is built, every backtest that's been run against it, and a full independent audit — including a correction that shipped, and a second correction that was investigated and deliberately not shipped once it turned out to be unnecessary.

How it worksPower Score, in plain terms

Every team gets a single Power Score, built from three pieces:

  • Baseline (45%) — last season's win total, rescaled so 8.5 wins (.500) is zero.
  • Trajectory (35%) — hand-scored Need-Fill (did the team fix its biggest roster hole?) and Stability (QB certainty, health, off-field risk). Coaching continuity (Scheme) is scored and shown on the dashboard too, but as of this audit no longer counts toward Power Score — see below.
  • Regression (20%) — a luck adjustment: teams that won more games than their point differential says they "should have" get pulled back toward the mean, and vice versa.

Power Score differences convert directly to predicted point spreads on a 1-to-1 basis — a team with a 3-point higher Power Score than its opponent is projected as roughly a 3-point favorite, full stop, plus a fixed 2-point home-field edge for the home team. There's no extra scaling step between Power Score and the spread. That number then feeds a win-probability curve, a 20,000-season Monte Carlo simulation for win-total ranges, and a full playoff bracket simulation for division/conference/Super Bowl odds.

ValidationWhat's actually been tested

Three full seasons (2023–2025) were rebuilt as if the model were run each of those preseasons, then checked against what actually happened — pooled to 96 team-seasons for real statistical power. Every structural change to the formula since then (alternate weight splits, adding real offensive/defensive efficiency data, special teams, strength of schedule) was tested the same disciplined way: fit on two seasons, validate on the third, rotate — so a change only ships if it holds up on data it never saw. Three of four attempted additions were rejected this way, including one whose raw correlation looked like the strongest signal found anywhere in the project before a deeper check showed it was just re-detecting information the model already had.

Audit correctionWhat changed on 2026-07-24, and why

An independent technical audit ran a full statistical review — architecture, weight sensitivity, a new spread-calibration regression against real historical games, and a residual analysis looking for patterns the model was missing. It found two things worth acting on, and — just as important — caught a third thing before it shipped that would have made the model worse.

Shipped

Coaching continuity ("Scheme") no longer counts toward Power Score. Three independent checks agreed it was quietly hurting accuracy: removing it from the formula improved the season-level correlation from r=0.352 to r=0.382; a separate check (controlling for how many wins a team already had) found Scheme's apparent effect was really just re-detecting that struggling teams both fire coaches and bounce back some regardless of who's coaching; and a new game-by-game check found the same backwards pattern on both sides of the ball. The likely story: coaching changes cluster after bad seasons, and bad seasons partly recover on their own — Scheme's "continuity is good" read was partly just re-measuring information the model already had elsewhere. Scheme is still scored and still shown on team pages — it just no longer moves the number.

Investigated, not shipped

A separate fix to how Power Score converts to a point spread was tested and deliberately left alone. The audit's first pass found that one Power Score point was predicting about 50% too many real scoreboard points — a real, measurable pattern. But before shipping that fix, it was re-checked after the coaching-continuity fix above — and the spread problem had already disappeared on its own. Coaching continuity's oddly wide scoring range had been distorting Power Score's scale; fixing that fixed the spread conversion as a side effect. Shipping both fixes together would have overcorrected, making every predicted spread too conservative. This is exactly the kind of check that's easy to skip under time pressure, and skipping it here would have made the model measurably worse while looking like an improvement in isolation.

Re-validated, same day

The 45%/35%/20% Baseline/Trajectory/Regression weight split was re-tested against the new, coaching-continuity-excluded formula — and came back more solid than before, not less. This split was originally validated years' worth of testing ago against the old 3-part Trajectory; re-running the identical fit-on-two-seasons-validate-on-the-third check against the new 2-part version, the current weights now win 2 of 3 test folds outright (previously they'd only clearly won 1 of 3). The best alternative found by searching all the data at once beat the current weights by less than the normal season-to-season noise — the same signature that ruled out changing them the first time. No change made.

Home-field advantage (a flat 2 points) was checked against the same real-game data and came back well-calibrated — no change there. Full numbers, regression tables, and code citations for both the audit and the correction are documented in the pipeline's own README.

In-season updatesThe preseason call, permanently preserved — then updated weekly

As of 2026-07-24 this model is no longer preseason-only. The preseason call is frozen the moment it's made and never overwritten again — that's the permanent starting point you'll always see on the Season Trend tab, however the year goes. From there, re-running the pipeline during the season blends real results in:

  • Baseline and Regression blend toward the season-to-date record as real games are played, gradually outweighing last year's record the same way a smart preseason bettor would update — backtested against three real seasons to pick the blending speed, not guessed at. Bye weeks are handled automatically since the blend is driven by games actually played, not the calendar.
  • Trajectory (roster needs, stability) stays frozen at its preseason value all season. That's deliberate — it's the preseason judgment call being graded, not a number that should quietly change its own answer. Real in-season events (an injury, a lost starter) show up through the Key-Person Dependency card and the injury watch list instead.
  • Played games count as played, not simulated. The Cone of Certainty and playoff odds credit real results directly and only simulate what's left — checked against the entire, already-completed 2025 season: simulated final win totals matched the real standings exactly for 30 of 32 teams, and the other 2 were off by exactly half a win, fully explained by the one real tie game that season.

Found and fixed along the way: three teams (Seahawks, Titans, Vikings) were previously showing 0.0% across every playoff-odds column — not because of anything about those teams, but because of a real bug in how the season simulator counted games, which surfaced while double-checking this feature's own math. All three now show real, sane odds.

Weekly runs stay a manual step, matching how the rest of this pipeline (and the sibling CFB model) already works — re-run the pipeline, review the numbers, redeploy. No auto-updating in the background.

Known limitsWhat this model doesn't do

  • It doesn't ingest betting lines or live injury news the way a market price does. Vegas closing lines, checked against this model on 816 real games, are noticeably more accurate — expected, since they price in everything up to kickoff and this model only updates when the pipeline is re-run by hand.
  • Need-Fill, Stability, and (still-displayed) Scheme are hand-scored judgment, not formulas. They're backed by written research citations, but two analysts reading the same news could reasonably land on adjacent scores.
  • Key-Person Dependency (the "what if the starting QB goes down" number) is only fully trustworthy for QB. The same league-wide method applied to Edge/Safety/LB came back unreliable for diagnosed reasons (modern substitution packages make "missed the game" hard to isolate from "coach called for a different personnel package") — reported honestly with a confidence flag, not smoothed over.
  • The 45%/35%/20% weight split hasn't been re-checked for a world where Baseline updates mid-season. It was re-validated when coaching continuity was removed from Trajectory, but not yet for this.
Every number on this page is backtested or measured against real historical data (nflverse game results and closing lines, 2023–2025) — nothing here is an estimate standing in for something unmeasured. Full technical detail, regression tables, and file-level code citations live in the pipeline repository's README.
